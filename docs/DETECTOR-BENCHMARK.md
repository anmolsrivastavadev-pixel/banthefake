# AI-image detector benchmark

This is the evidence behind the detector shipped in `app/scan/model.py`. It covers the dataset, how it was split,
what each candidate scored, and the rule the app uses. It also lists what the numbers cannot tell you.

The research workspace (`research/detect/`) is git-ignored because it holds about 1,300 downloaded images. This
document records the results. The last section explains how to rebuild the workspace and re-run everything.

## Shipped rule

The app runs two pinned CPU models on every image and every sampled video frame:

| Role | Model | Revision | Licence | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H, primary | [`haywoodsloan/ai-image-detector-deploy`](https://huggingface.co/haywoodsloan/ai-image-detector-deploy) | `4ae1822603cb1ec980c217cfde1ef1b8e0477a16` | Apache-2.0 (model card) | SwinV2, softmax over `artificial`/`real` |
| C, secondary | [`buildborderless/CommunityForensics-DeepfakeDet-ViT`](https://huggingface.co/buildborderless/CommunityForensics-DeepfakeDet-ViT) | `ac6ee457bea904a373065754107451793b56db00` | MIT (model card) | ViT-S, sigmoid of a single logit |

| Tier | Condition (per image or frame) |
|---|---|
| **Likely AI-generated** | H >= 0.98 **and** C >= 0.05 |
| **Possible signs of AI** | otherwise, H >= 0.98 **or** C >= 0.5 |
| No strong signal | everything else |

Extra rules in the app:

- **Low-colour images.** Black-and-white and low-colour images never get the "likely" verdict. They get
  "Detector unreliable for this image" instead.
- **One model fails to load.** The other model still runs, but its result is capped at "possible". The report
  adds an info signal saying so.
- **`BTF_MODEL_ENSEMBLE=off`.** Only C runs, with its previous production thresholds: 0.85 for "likely" and
  0.5 for "possible".
- **Videos.** The app decides each sampled frame with the rule above. The clip then gets the tier that a
  majority of its most suspicious half of frames reaches. With 8 frames, 3 must be "likely" for the clip to be
  "likely".

The rule was set using only the calibration split, as was the "possible" tier. The locked test split was used
only to confirm the result.

## Dataset

All 723 originals come from Wikimedia Commons, through exact category roots (`source_category`). The manifest is
`research/detect/data/manifest.csv`. Each row records:

- the source page URL, page id, revision id and timestamp
- author and licence
- the image's SHA-256 and perceptual hash (`dhash`)
- `label_evidence`, the categories and licence templates that justify the label
- `audit_status` and `reviewer`
- `duplicate_group`, which links near-duplicates

`bench.py` refuses to run in three cases:

- a file's bytes do not match its SHA-256
- an AI-labelled original has not been audited as `approved`
- a near-duplicate group spans two labels

### Audit process

- **Real images** come from curated Commons categories (Featured/Quality pictures, maps, diagrams). They were
  accepted under a written category policy (`reviewer = category-policy-v1`, 529 originals).
- **AI images** come from Commons "Images generated by …" categories, a source that is noisy. Every AI
  candidate went onto contact sheets (`audit.py` → `research/detect/audit/audit_NN.jpg`) and was checked against
  both its pixels and its source-page evidence. The reviewer column reads
  `agent-assisted (visual + source evidence)`. Of 194 candidates, 169 were approved and 25 rejected. Rejected
  candidates included logos and diagrams with only a small generated element, real photos that had only been
  AI-edited or AI-enhanced, and derived images such as edge-detection output.
- **Near-duplicates** were grouped by perceptual hash (`duplicate_group`). A group always lands in one split.

## Split method

`bench.py split_roots(rows, seed=7)` splits by source group, not by file:

- Originals are stratified by `(label, generator or source_key)`.
- Generators with fewer than 4 source groups are pooled within their label.
- Each stratum is shuffled with seed 7 and split 40% train, 30% calibration, 30% locked test.
- Degraded variants follow their parent (`partition_rows`), so a variant never leaks across splits.

| Split | Originals | Real (`real`/`real_bw`/`real_graphic`) | AI |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| train | 281 | 212 | 69 |
| calibration | 209 | 160 (99/30/31) | 49: MJ 26, DALL-E 8, Flux 4, GPT-image 4, SD 4, Imagen 2, Firefly 1 |
| locked test | 208 | 157 (98/30/29) | 51: MJ 28, DALL-E 7, Flux 4, GPT-image 4, SD 5, Imagen 3 |

The train split is used only to fit the stacking ensemble (`ensemble_stack`). Thresholds come from calibration
originals. All headline numbers below are measured on the locked test.

## Why an agreement rule, not H alone

H on its own at 0.98 has the best recall. But on the locked test it called 2 of 157 real originals "likely AI":
an architecture photo (H 0.987) and a black-and-white photo (H 0.990). On calibration it did the same to a night
photo (H 0.989) and a graphic (H 0.9985). C scored all four at 0.0003 or below. Requiring C >= 0.05 removes
them. The cost is some recall: on the test split, H alone catches 46 of 51 AI images and the agreement rule
catches 39.

Columns, in order:

- **Real FP**: real originals (all three real labels) that reach the tier.
- **95% upper**: Wilson upper bound on that rate.
- **Degraded real FP**: degraded variants of real images that reach the tier, within the same split.

| Rule | Split | Real FP | 95% upper | AI recall | Midjourney | Degraded real FP |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Likely: H>=0.98 AND C>=0.05** | calibration | 0/160 (0.0%) | 2.3% | 36/49 (73.5%) | 21/26 | 0/111 |
| **Likely: H>=0.98 AND C>=0.05** | test | 0/157 (0.0%) | 2.4% | 39/51 (76.5%) | 24/28 | 0/120 |
| **Possible or higher: likely OR H>=0.98 OR C>=0.5** | calibration | 2/160 (1.2%) | 4.4% | 41/49 (83.7%) | 23/26 | 0/111 |
| **Possible or higher: likely OR H>=0.98 OR C>=0.5** | test | 2/157 (1.3%) | 4.5% | 47/51 (92.2%) | 26/28 | 1/120 |
| H>=0.98 alone | calibration | 2/160 (1.2%) | 4.4% | 39/49 (79.6%) | 22/26 | 0/111 |
| H>=0.98 alone | test | 2/157 (1.3%) | 4.5% | 46/51 (90.2%) | 26/28 | 1/120 |

| C>=0.85 alone (previous production) | test | 0/157 (0.0%) | 2.4% | 29/51 (56.9%) | 18/28 | 0/120 |

Across all 402 degraded real variants (every split), "likely" fires on 0 and "possible or higher" on 2. On the
168 degraded AI variants, "likely" catches 90: JPEG q60 23/56, resize 29/56, screenshot 38/56. "Possible or
higher" catches 121. The previous production rule (C >= 0.85) caught 69.

Locked-test "likely" recall by generator: Midjourney 24/28, DALL-E 6/7, Stable Diffusion 4/5, Flux 2/4,
GPT-image 2/4, Imagen/Gemini 1/3. "Possible or higher": Midjourney 26/28, DALL-E 7/7, SD 5/5, Flux 4/4,
GPT-image 3/4, Imagen/Gemini 2/3.

### Choosing the "possible" tier

The target was a real-image rate of 10% or less at "possible or higher", measured on calibration originals of
all three real labels. Every candidate in the grid below meets that target. The chosen rule (H >= 0.98 OR
C >= 0.5) has the lowest real rate among candidates with comparable recall (2/160, 1.2%). Lowering the H
threshold adds a few AI images for more real ones. "Possible" stays a meaningful hint rather than noise.

| Calibration only | Real at possible+ | AI at possible+ |
|---|---:|---:|
| H>=0.5 or C>=0.5 | 7/160 (4.4%) | 44/49 |
| H>=0.9 or C>=0.5 | 4/160 (2.5%) | 44/49 |
| H>=0.95 or C>=0.5 | 3/160 (1.9%) | 42/49 |
| **H>=0.98 or C>=0.5 (shipped)** | **2/160 (1.2%)** | **41/49** |
| H>=0.98 or C>=0.85 | 2/160 (1.2%) | 40/49 |

Locked-test confirmation of the shipped tier: 2/157 real (1.3%, Wilson upper 4.5%) and 47/51 AI (92.2%).

The benchmark scores the raw rule. In the app, low-colour images additionally never reach "likely". For these
numbers that only matters for black-and-white images, and there it can only lower the false-positive rate.

## Limitations

- **Small test set.** The locked test has only 51 AI images (28 Midjourney) and 157 real originals. The
  bootstrap intervals above are wide, and per-generator numbers for Flux, GPT-image and Imagen rest on 3 to 4
  images each.
- **0 false positives is not 0%.** "Likely" produced 0 false positives out of 157 real originals, but the 95%
  Wilson upper bound is about 2.4%. Treat the real false-positive rate as "probably under 2.4%", not zero.
- **Commons images may not match social media.** These are curated, mostly high-quality photographs, diagrams
  and maps. Images shared on social media are recompressed, cropped, filtered, overlaid with text or
  screenshotted, sometimes repeatedly. The degraded variants cover only three simple transforms. Real-world
  error rates, especially false positives on heavily processed photos, may be higher.
- **GPT-image recall is weak.** Calibration "likely" caught 0/4 and the test caught 2/4. Newer generators that
  are not in either model's training data will be missed more often.
- **Training-data overlap is unknown.** H was trained with AutoTrain on data that is not published. Some
  Commons AI images may be in its training set, which would inflate its recall here.
- **No adversarial tests.** Nobody tried to evade the detectors with added noise, re-photographing, upscalers,
  or images built to fool them. A motivated adversary can probably push either model below its threshold.
- **Partial desaturation.** In a reviewer probe, partially desaturating an AI image (for example keeping 15% of
  its colour, which is not caught by the low-colour guard) can drop the secondary model's score, which can lower a "likely"
  result to "possible" or "none". Fully greyscale and low-colour images are excluded from AI verdicts.
- **Whole-image classifiers.** These models do not detect face swaps, inpainting of a small region, or lip-sync
  in video.
- **Labels can still be wrong.** The AI labels rest on Commons categories plus an agent-assisted visual and
  source audit, not on creator attestation.

## How to re-run

The research workspace is not committed. To rebuild it:

```bash
# 1. Python env with the ml extra
uv sync --extra ml

# 2. Collect images from Commons into research/detect/data/ (writes manifest.csv), then audit the AI labels
# --degrade adds the jpeg_q60 / resize_512 / screenshot_png variants
.venv/bin/python research/detect/collect.py --degrade
# write contact sheets, review them, then apply the approved/rejected decisions
.venv/bin/python research/detect/audit.py --sheets research/detect/audit
.venv/bin/python research/detect/audit.py --apply decisions.csv --reviewer "<name>"

# 3. Fetch pinned weights once, then score offline (results are cached per model revision and image hash)
export HF_HOME=$PWD/research/detect/hf_cache
.venv/bin/python research/detect/fetch_models.py
HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1 .venv/bin/python research/detect/bench.py --models current haywoodsloan ateeqq prithiv_v2

# 4. Uncertainty analysis, and the shipped agreement rule from the score caches (no model run needed)
.venv/bin/python research/detect/analysis.py
.venv/bin/python research/detect/agreement.py
```

`bench.py` writes `report.md` and `scores_<model>_<revision>.csv`. `analysis.py` writes `analysis/analysis.md`.
`agreement.py` prints the agreement-rule table above. All of them use the seed-7 source-grouped split. Commons
pages change over time, so a fresh collection will not match these files exactly. The manifest's SHA-256
column pins the set measured here.
