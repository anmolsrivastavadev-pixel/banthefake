# BanTheFake: a practical guide to the UK campaign

A plain-English, practical guide for students running the BanTheFake campaign on the UK street. Everything here comes from the campaign site and the campaign materials; nothing is invented.

## The ask

The campaign's demands, from the "Our demands" section of the campaign site (`static/index.html`):

- **Ban non-consensual deepfakes** — Make it illegal to create or share sexual or defamatory deepfakes of real people without their consent, and give victims the right to sue.
- **Fast mandatory takedowns** — Require platforms to remove reported non-consensual deepfakes within 48 hours and keep them from being re-uploaded.
- **Label all synthetic media** — Require AI tools to attach content credentials and watermarks, and require platforms to show them.
- **Hold the tools accountable** — Ban apps built to "undress" people or clone voices without consent, and remove them from app stores.
- **Protect elections** — Ban deceptive deepfakes of candidates in the run-up to an election.

The campaign is not against AI. It is against using someone's face or voice without consent to harm, deceive or exploit them.

## Street campaigning

**The 10-second opener** — two variants:

- "Would you be OK with someone making a fake intimate image of you from your photos?"
- "AI can copy your voice from a short clip and ring your family asking for money"

**The 20-second signing flow** — point people to the QR code on your materials, let them read before they sign, thank them, and drop the pressure. The sign itself is labelled "Scan to sign - 20 seconds".

**Where to stand** — public pavements are the usual spot. On private property such as shopping centres and stations, ask permission first.

**Go with a friend or an adult.** Use a tablet or the QR code instead of paper sign-up sheets — paper names and emails are personal data under UK GDPR.

**Three common objections, with one-line answers:**

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "AI is the future, you can't ban it." | "We're not banning AI — just using someone's face or voice without consent." |
| "Isn't this already illegal?" | "Sharing intimate deepfakes is already a crime, but voice scams, takedowns and election fakes still have gaps." |
| "Is my data safe?" | "We never share your email and you can ask for deletion — see the privacy page." |

## Print materials

The street campaign print materials are generated by `campaign/print/make_print.py`:

```bash
uv run --no-project --with segno --with reportlab python campaign/print/make_print.py --url https://<your live site>
```

This writes an A4 sign, a sheet of four A6 flyers, a pitch cue card and a Parliament petition draft to
`campaign/print/out/`. The QR codes say "Scan to sign", so only print them once `--url` points at the live
site's petition, and test-scan a printed copy with a phone first.

## UK Parliament petition

A draft (also written to `campaign/print/out/parliament_petition_draft.md` by the print script):

**Draft title:** "Require 48-hour takedowns and ban non-consensual intimate deepfakes" (67/80 characters)

**What we want:** "Require platforms to remove reported non-consensual intimate deepfakes within 48 hours. Make creating or sharing them without consent a criminal offence for victims of every age, and require clear labels on AI-generated media." (226/300)

**More details:** "AI tools can turn ordinary photos into convincing intimate images without the person's consent. Victims need a fast, consistent route to removal, whatever their age. Current protections should cover both creating and sharing this material, while platforms should act on valid reports within 48 hours and preserve evidence for law enforcement. Clear, durable labels on AI-generated images, audio and video would also help people judge what they see and hear. The Government should set enforceable duties, proportionate penalties and an appeals process that protects lawful journalism, satire and art." (599/800)

The student must create and submit this petition themselves at <https://petition.parliament.uk/>. The petition site's own rules, checks and moderation apply — review the live rules before submitting, and note that acceptance is not guaranteed.

The Government responds to a petition at 10,000 signatures, and Parliament considers a debate at 100,000.

## Writing to your MP

Point people at the letter generator on the campaign site: fill in a few details and copy a ready-to-send letter to your representative (or to a platform or your school).

## Safety and wellbeing

Campaigners may meet people who have been targeted. Be kind, don't ask for details, and point them to Report Remove (under 18), Childline 0800 1111, or StopNCII.org.

If someone discloses that they made or shared an image, point them to Stop It Now UK on 0808 1000 900 and encourage them to tell a trusted adult.
