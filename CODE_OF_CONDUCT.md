# Code of Conduct

This is an original, project-specific code of conduct rather than a reproduction of Contributor Covenant 2.1.

## Our commitment

We want BanTheFake's project spaces to be safe, useful, and welcoming to people of every background, identity,
experience level, and point of view. Contributors, maintainers, and visitors are expected to treat one another
with respect and to remember that deceptive media can cause serious personal harm.

## Expected behavior

- Discuss ideas and evidence without insulting, threatening, or humiliating people.
- Give constructive feedback, accept correction, and make room for less experienced contributors.
- Respect privacy, consent, boundaries, and requests to remove personal information.
- Describe detector results and research limits honestly, including uncertainty and negative results.
- Keep disagreements focused on the work and take responsibility for harm caused by your conduct.

## Unacceptable behavior

Harassment, discrimination, sexual attention, threats, doxxing, stalking, sustained disruption, and publishing
someone's private information without permission are not allowed.

Do not share, request, or create non-consensual intimate media or deceptive media of real people in any project
space. This includes issues, pull requests, discussions, code, test fixtures, links, and private messages sent
as part of project participation. Reports must use descriptions and safe synthetic examples instead.

## Scope and enforcement

This code applies in repository spaces and whenever someone represents the project in public or private
project-related communication. Maintainers may edit or remove content, reject contributions, issue a warning,
temporarily restrict participation, or permanently ban someone, depending on the seriousness and pattern of
the conduct.

Report abusive GitHub content with GitHub's reporting controls and privately notify a maintainer when possible.
Do not start a public thread that repeats sensitive material. Maintainers will protect the reporter's privacy as
far as they reasonably can, review the available context, avoid conflicts of interest, and respond in a way
proportionate to the behavior.
