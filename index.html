The problem

Fake media causes real harm

We're not against AI. We're against using someone's likeness without their consent to hurt, deceive, or exploit them.

⚠

Non-consensual imagery

Most harmful deepfakes are sexual images of real people, often women and minors, made and shared without consent.

☏

Voice-clone scams

Scammers clone a relative's voice and call asking for money in a fake emergency. Their targets are often older people.

⚑

Political lies

Fake videos and robocalls of candidates spread faster than fact-checks can catch them, especially just before elections.

    If it happened to you

    Get it taken down

    It's not your fault. Save evidence first (screenshots, links, usernames, dates), then use these services.

    StopNCII.org ↗

    Adults: create a digital fingerprint of intimate images on your own device so partner platforms can block them. The images themselves are never uploaded.

    Take It Down (NCMEC) ↗

    For images of anyone under 18, including AI-generated ones. Free and anonymous.

    FBI IC3 (US) ↗

    Report voice-clone scams, extortion, and fraud that used fake media.

    CCRI Crisis Helpline ↗

    Free, confidential support for victims of image-based abuse.
    What we're asking for

    Our demands

    Ban non-consensual deepfakes

    Make it illegal to create or share sexual or defamatory deepfakes of real people without their consent, and give victims the right to sue.

    Fast mandatory takedowns

    Require platforms to remove reported non-consensual deepfakes within 48 hours and keep them from being re-uploaded.

    Label all synthetic media

    Require AI tools to attach content credentials and watermarks, and require platforms to show them.

    Hold the tools accountable

    Ban apps built to "undress" people or clone voices without consent, and remove them from app stores.

    Protect elections

    Ban deceptive deepfakes of candidates in the run-up to an election.

    FAQ

    Questions

    Are you trying to ban all AI?

    No. Face-swaps in films, satire that's clearly labeled, and accessibility uses like restoring a lost voice are fine. We oppose deepfakes made without consent that are used to harm or deceive.

    Can your scanner tell me for sure if something is fake?

    No, and no tool can. It reads hidden markers that many AI tools leave behind (like C2PA content credentials and generator settings) and flags signs of editing. If nothing is found, that doesn't mean the file is real.

    Do you upload my files?

    No. The scanner runs entirely in your browser. You can turn off your internet after the page loads and it still works.

    Isn't this already illegal?

    In some places, partly. The US TAKE IT DOWN Act (2025) targets non-consensual intimate images, including AI-generated ones, and the EU AI Act requires deepfakes to be labeled. Coverage still has big gaps, especially for voice clones, scams, and election content.
